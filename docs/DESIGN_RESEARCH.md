## Benestudio design synthesis

1. Original photography, appropriate image ratios, and project context create perceived quality more reliably than decorative UI.
2. Use contemporary sans typography, precise metadata, hairlines, and composition—not the generic beige/italic-serif luxury formula.
3. The homepage must establish studio, service, market, and next action quickly; photography cannot carry discovery alone.
4. Selected work should be curated and varied; full archives belong on the project index, with narrative detail pages.
5. Give visitors only a few deliberate CTA moments, all using one primary phrase: **Begin a Project**.
6. Use a dedicated project-application flow to qualify intent without making the portfolio feel like a sales page.
7. Make each page a distinct mobile composition, preserving scanability, touch targets, and natural image rhythm.
8. Animation should reveal, orient, or confirm; it must never obstruct content, scroll, keyboard use, or performance.

Research ratings are qualitative design assessments, not measured conversion data. Live conversion claims require consented analytics plus qualified-lead and booking feedback.

## 2026 conversion validation

Current template and intake references reinforce the BICS model: Framer’s current interior and architecture templates prioritize image-led portfolio pages, project/service detail, forms, and restrained motion; they are not a reason to copy generic template visual language. Current interior questionnaire examples consistently collect location/property, scope, budget, timing, decision-maker context, and a concise description. BICS therefore keeps a short staged inquiry rather than a generic contact form or an exhausting questionnaire.
